> **Status:** Non-normative research program. Its hypotheses remain
> under investigation unless and until accepted through the GSM Change Proposal
> process.

## 1. Program mandate

Systemics is the paradigm. GSM is a candidate systemics model — a generative,
definition-centric embodiment under test, not the paradigm itself.

This program investigates one question:

> What must a generative systemics model represent so that organization,
> purpose, regulation, observation, and adaptation remain accountable —
> including whether current GSM primitives, roles, and grammar are
> sufficient, excessive, or incomplete?

Current GSM is the baseline candidate, not a freeze. Research may propose
adding, splitting, merging, relocating, or withdrawing primitives, roles, or
grammar when the systemic consequences are explicit. A result that cannot
state its relation to purpose, variety, operational closure, recursion, or
viability is not ready, whether or not it fits current GSM.

The program seeks an explicit causal model without assuming its taxonomy. It
treats the governance chain as an explicit research object and uses teleology
as a flexible test, not as a causal category.

The following remain outside the mandate:

- quality scoring, ranking, or treating any Quality formula as adopted;
- a survey of systemics or governance theories for its own sake;
- implementation architecture, runtime topology, or processor design.

## 2. Program quality criteria

A chapter, case, or decision is adequate only when it satisfies these
criteria. They define program quality; they are not additional research
objects.

| ID | Criterion | Test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | Scientific method fidelity | Observation, problem, question, hypothesis, and finding remain distinct. The baseline records current knowledge only. |
| C2 | Intent–problem–question alignment | Intent frames the theme. Problems determine questions. Questions do not smuggle answers. |
| C3 | Falsifiability | Every live hypothesis names a discriminating test, a counterexample class, and a stop condition. |
| C4 | Evidence adequacy | Claims rest on cited artifacts, executed cases, or formal derivation. Method menus are not evidence. |
| C5 | Internal consistency | Frontmatter, maps, chapters, and decisions do not pre-answer what the protocol keeps open. |
| C6 | Systemics paradigm fidelity | Inquiry and results are judged first against the [systemics accounts](#6-systemics-paradigm). Current GSM is the candidate model, not the paradigm and not a freeze. |
| C7 | Level separation | Research method is not modeled as a GSM relation. Production, authorization, scope, and activation stay distinct unless a finding unifies them. |
| C8 | Decision hygiene | One decision is one fork. Duplicates are merged. Status is not a record role. Resolution requirements are usable. |
| C9 | Placement discipline | Core GSM, GSM ontology, or derived view is a result of tests, not a recurring paragraph. |
| C10 | Formalization discipline | Formal objects appear only when they constrain inquiry. Inventories do not replace results. |
| C11 | Workstream economy | Each chapter owns one explanatory burden. Overlap is declared, not recopied. |
| C12 | Program actionability | A next investigator can run the next test without inventing the method. |

Passing C1–C12 makes the program scientifically usable. It does not make a
result normative.

## 4. Research protocol

Each thematic chapter uses the same research logic. Its top-level sections
follow this order:

1. **Research intent and scope:** why the inquiry exists, what domain it
   covers, and what remains outside its boundary.
2. **Current state of knowledge:** only what authoritative GSM artifacts
   explicitly represent, what they make derivable, and what existing GSM
   ontology artifacts already define.
3. **Phenomena and research problems:** numbered `P-xx-n` records of
   observed phenomena, conceptual tensions, or representational gaps relative
   to that current state. Absence from GSM belongs here, not in the baseline.
4. **Research questions:** numbered `Q-xx-n` questions derived from stated
   problems. A question cites the problem it reduces.
5. **Candidate classifications and hypotheses:** numbered `H-xx-n`
   alternatives. Each hypothesis cites a question and names one falsifier.
6. **Method and evidence:** numbered `E-xx-n` evidence, including executed
   cases from the [corpus](cases.md). Evaluation menus without execution are
   not evidence.
7. **Findings and representation implications:** numbered `F-xx-n` results
   supported by stated evidence, including negative or inconclusive results.
   Placement is compared only for findings that constrain representation.
8. **Decisions and dependencies:** every normative fork links to the Decision
   Register.

Record prefixes are `CM` Causal Model, `GC` Governance Chain, `DP` DNA
Production, and `CASE` for corpus items.

Research records use the following roles. These are not interchangeable
states of one claim, and they are not Decision Register statuses:

| Record role | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Research intent | Purpose and scope of an inquiry; frames but does not determine its findings |
| Baseline statement | Documentary claim about what current authoritative GSM or GSM ontology artifacts represent or derive |
| Phenomenon | Reproducibly identified behavior, pattern, consequence, or conceptual occurrence requiring explanation |
| Research problem | Warranted discrepancy, contradiction, explanatory gap, or representational gap |
| Research question | Bounded question whose answer would reduce the research problem |
| Hypothesis | Testable candidate explanation, relation, classification, or solution |
| Evidence | Documentary, formal, empirical, operational, or counterfactual material used to assess a hypothesis |
| Finding | Result supported by the stated method and evidence, including negative or inconclusive results |
| Candidate outcome | Finding mature enough to assess for representation and promotion |

An observation is a means of producing evidence about a phenomenon. A Need or
requirement is not an observation: it is a normative or design conclusion that
requires an identified subject, purpose, condition, rationale, and supporting
evidence.

Preferred terms in this program: *Adverse condition* rather than *Pain*;
*role signature*, *schema*, or *relation* rather than *contract*, except for
a GSM Change Proposal; *identity*, *ownership*, *scope*, *activation*, or
*operationalization* rather than *capital*; *governance chain* as a research
object, not as something GSM already requires. Named relation families are
instruments, not an accepted taxonomy.

## 6. Systemics paradigm

These accounts are how the program thinks. They are not a late checklist and
not a competing ontology. Promotion gate 2 uses the same clash tests.

| Account | Systemic content | GSM contact | Clash test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose as identity | Organization is identified by why it exists, not by a container label. | `Structure.purpose`; `GOV-D08` | Two Structures sharing one purpose collide with purpose as unique identity. |
| Teleology as test | Purpose, function, and rule must do distinct work. | `Purpose -> Function -> Rule` | If purpose changes and the justified evaluation or Directive does not, purpose is idle. |
| Requisite variety | Distinct causal contributions must remain distinguishable. | one Norm per qualifier versus many Mechanisms | A single assertion that hides distinct contributions fails the variety test. |
| Operational closure / autopoiesis | A System produces and evaluates the definitions that constitute it. | owner-local Compliance; DNA production; `GOV-D20` | External Compliance with no owner-local path fails claimed self-production. |
| Recursion without nested Structures | Recursion is DNA targeting, not subsystem containment. | flat Structures; `GOV-D07` | A required containment hierarchy contradicts claimed flatness. |
| Beer as inspiration, not embedding | Viable-system regulation (policy, regulation, supervision) informs G/R/S. GSM does not store S1–S5 or nested viable systems. | `SH-20`, `H-GC-13`; `gsm.puml` VSM notes | Identifying G/R/S with S1–S5, or requiring nested viable systems, imports a second topology. |
| Viability as derived balance | Quality is value against cost and risk, not a compensating score. | Quality, Value, Cost, Risk; `GOV-D32` | A scalar that compensates a catastrophic constraint fails viability. |
| Definition of the state | The primary regulated variable is the definition of the state, not the state alone. | DNA grammar; Ascription lifecycle | A model that regulates only observed state and never definitions is not this candidate. |
| Emergent Systemness | System is not stored. Reflexive Mechanisms yield Systemness. | `GOV-D01` | Treating System as a primitive class without reflexive production fails emergence. |

A hypothesis that cannot state how it would fail one of these tests is not
ready for promotion. The same accounts constrain problem formation, not only
promotion.

## 7. Cross-cutting method

- Labels do not prove roles; causal signatures and topology do.
- Definition identity and Ascription snapshot identity are not interchangeable.
- Declared topology and observed operation are different facts.
- Governance scope remains explicit across every Structure boundary.
- Directive, Norm, and Ascription tempos remain distinct.

## 8. Promotion gates

A candidate outcome becomes eligible for a GSM Change Proposal only when it
passes all applicable gates:

1. **Semantic adequacy:** terms, relations, scope, direction, cardinality, and
   alternatives are explicit.
2. **Systemics coherence:** the outcome survives the [systemics
   paradigm](#6-systemics-paradigm).
3. **Placement:** the case for core GSM, GSM ontology, or a derived view is
   explicit and compared with the alternatives.
4. **Formalizability:** the outcome can be expressed through a metamodel,
   schema, expression form, derivation rule, or another testable form.
5. **Empirical and operational adequacy:** executed examples, counterexamples,
   evidence requirements, and observable consequences are identified.
6. **Conformance impact:** normative obligations and non-normative analyses are
   distinguished, including what would constitute failure.
7. **Traceability:** supporting findings, rejected alternatives, affected GSM
   artifacts, and dependent decision IDs are recorded.

Passing these gates supports proposal readiness. It does not itself make a
research outcome normative.
